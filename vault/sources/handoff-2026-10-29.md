## 3. Decisions and the reasoning behind them

- **Subset levels by azimuth step (3°, 6°, 12°, 24°, 45° → 600/300/150/75/40 images on 5 rings)** instead of 100/50/25/12.5/6.25%: percentages of 1,800 are not integers at the low end, 1°-apart views are near-duplicates, and a uniform step keeps coverage constant so only density changes. L1–L4 are nested. If L1 is too slow on the CPU, L2 becomes the top level.
- **±90° cameras:** if they are straight top/bottom views, rotating them gives the same picture spun around (no new viewpoint). Then use one image per pole ring (`pole_rings` in the config) — verify from the files.
- **Depth maps for evaluation only, never as input.** Feeding the ground truth in and measuring error against it is circular (error ≈ 0 by construction); it would also turn the study into RGB-D fusion, which needs a depth sensor in real life and contradicts "cost-effective, camera-only". The team asked and accepted this.
- **Why evaluation is not trivial:** a depth map is one camera's per-pixel distance, not a 3D model. Building a 3D reference needs (1) the depth encoding/units, (2) camera intrinsics, (3) camera poses; and COLMAP output has arbitrary scale/rotation/position, so it must be aligned (camera centres → similarity transform, refined with ICP) before Chamfer/Hausdorff/RMSE/F-score mean anything. If depth is normalized 8-bit or camera parameters are missing, fall back to comparing in depth-map space (Option B in `04-Methodology`). Hypothesis to check: depth is 8-bit (the companion paper reports RMSE 37.35, a 0–255 scale).
- **COLMAP (CPU) + OpenMVS (CPU):** the laptop has Intel Iris Xe, no CUDA; COLMAP's dense stereo needs CUDA. Same matcher and settings at every level; fix the matcher after the pilot.
- **Virtual museum is compulsory and web-based.** License CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 → museum must credit the dataset authors and the Virtual Hampson Museum, non-commercial, same license for derived models.
- **Python 3.13 kept** (Open3D 0.20.0 has 3.13 wheels).
